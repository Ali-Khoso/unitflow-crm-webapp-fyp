# Problem statement and objectives

## The real-world problem

Small and medium home-service businesses may manage **customer enquiries, sales follow-ups, service appointments, technician assignments and invoices** through separate software or manual tools. Such fragmentation can lead to:
- Slow response to new enquiries and missed follow-ups.
- Copying customer/property/job details between systems.
- Poor visibility into a job's progress from initial lead to invoice.
- Difficulty reusing historical information about customers, properties and equipment.

**Our proposed response:** Build and evaluate a connected, property-aware CRM and field-operations web application with configurable automation.

## AC repair example

A homeowner requests repair of an AC unit. Office staff need to acknowledge the request, book a visit, prepare an estimate and assign a technician. With connected records, the request, customer, property, equipment, approved estimate and service job should stay linked.

## Proposed FYP objectives

1. Design a multi-tenant customer/property/service data model.
2. Implement tenant-aware CRM, estimates, jobs and technician interfaces.
3. Build configurable graph workflows, timers, branches, retries and execution logs.
4. Connect communication events and limited AI-assisted structured extraction.
5. Evaluate reliability, latency, extraction accuracy, usability and access controls.

## What we are *not* claiming

Products such as Housecall Pro, Jobber, Zoho and GoHighLevel already implement many overlapping features. Our academic contribution is **how we design, integrate and evaluate a focused MVP**, not the invention of CRM or an assertion that competitors lack automation.

Commercial advantages such as affordability, localization and ease of use are **hypotheses to test with users**, not established findings.
